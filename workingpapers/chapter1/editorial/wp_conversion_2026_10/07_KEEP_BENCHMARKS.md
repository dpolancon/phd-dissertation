# 07 — KEEP Benchmarks

These passages are the closest existing examples of the target voice. Later editing should make weaker passages sound more like these, rather than imposing a generic external style.

## KB-01 — Capacity-utilization controversy (§2.3, pp.5–6)

**Why keep:** The paragraph defines a real debate, names the two positions, and gives each side a mechanism without unnecessary decoration. It uses field-specific language naturally.

**Editing lesson:** Preserve substantive heterodox vocabulary; reduce rhetorical framing around it.

## KB-02 — GPIM construction (§4.2, p.22)

**Anchor:** “To reconstruct the gross capital stock, we use the Generalized Perpetual Inventory Method…”

**Why keep:** Concrete data provenance, transformations, and reproducibility choices. The paragraph tells the reader exactly what is done.

**Editing lesson:** This is the register to imitate in technical sections: source → operation → reason → appendix pointer.

## KB-03 — Raw post-war trends (§4.2, p.23), first half

**Anchor:** “Between 1947 and 2011… real corporate gross capital stock grew… output… employment…”

**Why keep:** Magnitudes come first and give the econometric exercise historical grounding.

**Boundary:** The final sentences currently over-promote the descriptive evidence into a structural verdict; keep the empirical beginning, calibrate the ending.

## KB-04 — Baseline reconstruction (§4.4, p.27)

**Anchor:** “The closest reconstruction yields an elasticity of θ=0.72 from an ARDL(2,4)…”

**Why keep:** It states the estimate, model, comparison, and exact F-bounds result quickly.

**Boundary:** Merge with the duplicate preceding paragraph and remove “highly significant/robustly” from the follow-up.

## KB-05 — S1 focal-model comparison (§4.5, p.29)

**Anchor:** ARDL(3,3), θ=0.92 versus RICOMP ARDL(1,2), θ=0.65.

**Why keep:** Concrete model comparison makes “specification sensitivity” visible without needing rhetorical emphasis.

## KB-06 — Full vs pre-2008 bivariate sample (§4.7, p.38)

**Why keep:** The paragraph gives the full-sample result, then immediately shows the boundary condition (1947–2007), which is exactly how a critical replication should present fragility.

**Editing lesson:** Prefer this falsification-oriented register to categorical “proves/rejects” language.

## KB-07 — Limitations paragraph (§5, p.39)

**Anchor:** “The single-sector bounding and the time-invariant parameterization of θ represent the two primary analytical limitations…”

**Why keep:** Specific limitations are tied directly to the model rather than presented as a generic caveat dump.

**Editing lesson:** This is a good conclusion register; trim clauses, but preserve the structure.
